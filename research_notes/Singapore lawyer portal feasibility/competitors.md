# Competitive landscape: Singapore lawyer-finder portal

Method note: WebFetch was blocked by the network egress proxy for nearly all target domains (lawsociety.org.sg, sso.agc.gov.sg, thelawyerguide.sg, sgfindlawyer.com, singaporelegaladvice.com, vulcanpost.com, sra.org.uk). Findings below come from search-result snippets only, not full-page reads. Treat pricing/traction as unverified unless stated. Where I could not source something it is under Gaps.

## Existing Singapore platforms that list or match lawyers

### Takeaway
At least six Singapore lawyer directories/matchers exist (official regulator search, Law Society paid directory, and several private portals: SingaporeLegalAdvice.com, The Lawyer Guide, SgFindLawyer, That Lawyer Friend, SingaporeFREELegalAdvice). The market is fragmented with no dominant consumer brand evident; the oldest private player (SingaporeLegalAdvice.com, 2011) claims 200,000+ monthly visitors.

### Cited Findings
- The Legal Services Regulatory Authority (Ministry of Law) runs a "Search for a Lawyer or Law Practice" listing all solicitors with a current practising certificate plus registered foreign lawyers and law practice entities; searchable by name, practice area and type of practice. This is the authoritative verification source, not a marketing channel. — [MinLaw LSRA search](https://eservices.mlaw.gov.sg/lsra/search-lawyer-or-law-firm/)
- The Law Society's "Find a Featured Lawyer/Law Firm" lists only practices that chose to advertise on the site; it is not exhaustive and the listings are paid advertising. The Law Society also sells advertising and a specialist directory, and runs a "Community Law" directory by practice area. (Note: I could not verify the user's name "Find a Law Practice"; the live names found are "Find a Featured Lawyer/Law Firm".) — [Law Society find a lawyer](https://www.lawsociety.org.sg/find-a-lawyer/), [Featured page](https://www.lawsociety.org.sg/find-a-featured-lawyer-law-firm/), [Advertise](https://www.lawsociety.org.sg/advertise/), [Community Law](https://www.lawsociety.org.sg/find-a-lawyer/community-law/)
- SingaporeLegalAdvice.com: founded 2011 by Yuk Lun Chan; claims 200,000+ monthly visitors, 600+ article Learning Centre, "Find a Lawyer", "Call a Lawyer" and WillMaker services. Founder listed in Asia Law Portal's 2017 "30 people to watch". Visitor figure is self-reported via Asia Law Portal interview/profile. — [Asia Law Portal](https://asialawportal.com/singaporelegaladvice-com-a-one-stop-platform-for-lawyers-and-legal-consumers/), [site](https://singaporelegaladvice.com/), [About](https://singaporelegaladvice.com/about/)
- The Lawyer Guide (thelawyerguide.sg): says it lets users find, compare and review lawyers, references 7,000+ lawyers, and forwards requests to participating lawyers who contact the user with non-binding offers (a lead-forwarding model). — [thelawyerguide.sg](https://thelawyerguide.sg/)
- SgFindLawyer: independent directory/legal info site across seven practice areas (divorce, criminal, wills, probate, employment, personal injury, civil litigation); featured lawyers must hold a current practising certificate. — [sgfindlawyer.com](https://sgfindlawyer.com/)
- That Lawyer Friend (TLF): free "AI Legal Concierge" matching clients to lawyers from a plain-English description. Claims: vetted lawyers, "no self-listed profiles, no pay-to-be-featured", and that it "does not sit in the middle of the engagement nor take a cut from the fees". These are self-descriptions; how it monetises is not stated in what I saw. — [tlfconnect.com](https://tlfconnect.com/)
- SingaporeFREELegalAdvice: free portal for advice from lawyers on criminal, family/divorce, civil litigation and corporate law. — [singaporefreelegaladvice.com](https://www.singaporefreelegaladvice.com/)
- Other listed resources surfaced: LawOnline Singapore directories, expatliving.sg guide, ask.legal blog, Best Lawyers Singapore ranking. — [LawOnline](https://lawonline.com.sg/directories/), [Best Lawyers](https://www.bestlawyers.com/singapore)
- MinLaw publishes a page of legal support resources for the public (legal aid etc.). — [MinLaw](https://www.mlaw.gov.sg/news/announcements/legal-support-resources-available-to-members-of-the-public/)

### Inferences
- Consumer legal-finder space is crowded with small, SEO-led portals; differentiation would need to be verified data, UX, or vertical niche rather than mere listing.
- The Law Society's own directory is a paid ad product, so "paid listing" per se is an accepted model in Singapore when run by the Society.

## Business models and Singapore conduct-rule compliance

### Takeaway
Singapore's Legal Profession (Professional Conduct) Rules 2015 bar touting and paying referrers any commission or consideration (rule 39), and bar businesses that unfairly attract business (rule 38). A pure subscription/flat advertising fee model is the safest read; per-lead or per-matter success fees are the highest risk. I found no authoritative Law Society ruling approving or rejecting any third-party pay-per-lead platform.

### Cited Findings
- Rule 39(1): a legal practitioner or law practice must not tout for business or do anything likely to lead to a reasonable inference that it was for touting. Rule 39(2): where a client is referred by another person, the practitioner must not reward the referror by any commission or other form of consideration. — search summary of [Professional Conduct Rules 2015 (SSO)](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/SL/LPA1966-S706-2015?ProvIds=P15-); see also [Law Society PD 7.4.3 on referrals from third parties](https://www.lawsociety.org.sg/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/35.-Warrant-To-Act-Letter-of-Engagement-and-Referrals-from-Third-Parties-PD-7.4.3.pdf)
- Rule 40: conveyancing referral agreements must be in writing, with no commission to referror and publicity must not suggest conveyancing is free. — same sources
- Rule 38: must not engage in a business that derogates from the dignity of the profession or is likely to unfairly attract business. — same sources
- The Law Society's own paid "featured" directory demonstrates that fixed advertising fees are compatible. — [Law Society](https://www.lawsociety.org.sg/find-a-lawyer/)
- Commentary exists on finder's fees and referral agreements generally in Singapore (not lawyer-specific). — [SingaporeLegalAdvice finder's fee article](https://singaporelegaladvice.com/law-articles/legal-offer-accept-finders-fee-singapore/)
- The Law Society has guidance note on ethics and IT (GN 6.1.1). — [GN 6.1.1](https://law-society-singapore-prod.s3.ap-southeast-1.amazonaws.com/2020/03/121.-Ethics-and-Information-Technology-GN-6.1.1.pdf)

### Inferences
- Pay-per-lead or revenue share could be characterised as "consideration to a referror" under rule 39(2); a flat subscription for advertising is lower risk. This is an inference, not a verified ruling; legal advice from a Singapore practitioner is needed.
- TLF's marketing ("no pay-to-be-featured", no cut of fees) suggests private platforms perceive compliance sensitivity.
- No evidence found that any listed competitor has been sanctioned.

### Gaps
- Exact text of rules 38-40 and any amendments (SSO fetch blocked; I only have secondary summaries). The 2015 rules may have been amended; not verified.
- No Law Society guidance specifically on lead-gen marketplaces found.
- How each competitor monetises (subscription vs per-lead) not confirmed.

## Analogues in other jurisdictions

### Takeaway
In the US, Avvo's fixed-fee Legal Services (lawyer paid a "marketing fee" per matter) was found by at least eight state bars to breach fee-sharing/referral rules and was shut down July 2018; LegalMatch (subscription, lawyers pay) was held in 2019 to be an unregistered lawyer referral service under California law but survived by certifying. UK SRA allows lead-gen if payment is for marketing, not referrals, with disclosure, but bans referral fees in personal injury.

### Cited Findings
- Avvo Legal Services shut down 31 July 2018 after ethics opinions; eight states (NY, OH, PA, SC, NJ, UT, VA pending, IN) said lawyers could not participate. — [Hinshaw](https://www.hinshawlaw.com/newsroom-updates-avvo-shuts-down-its-legal-services-product-in-wake-of-ethics-opinions-warning-attorneys-not-to-participate.html), [LawNext](https://www.lawnext.com/2018/07/avvo-legal-services-shut.html), [ABA Journal](https://www.abajournal.com/news/article/accordingly_we_have_decided_to_discontinue_avvo_legal_services/)
- NY State Bar Opinion 1132 found fees paid to Avvo violate referral, fee-splitting and advertising rules (improper vouching, referral fees, sharing with non-lawyer); SC found the per-service marketing fee was a contingency-type share; NJ similar. — [NYSBA 1132](https://nysba.org/ethics-opinion-1132/), [ABA Journal](https://www.abajournal.com/news/article/ethics_opinion_on_fee_sharing_is_bad_news_for_avvos_legal_referral_service)
- Avvo acquired by Internet Brands, announced Jan 2018; founder and four execs departed. Avvo now part of Martindale-Avvo, which sells advertising and a pay-per-lead service (Martindale-Nolo). — [LawNext](https://www.lawnext.com/2018/01/breaking-avvo-acquired-internet-brands.html), [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2018/avvo-founder-mark-britton-4-top-execs-depart-internet-brands-acquisition-closes/), [Martindale-Avvo FAQ](https://www.martindale-avvo.com/faq/)
- ABA Law Practice magazine discusses impact of lead generation. — [ABA](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/law_practice/publications/law_practice_magazine/2021/ma21/saam/)
- Jackson v. LegalMatch (Cal. Ct. App. 2019): LegalMatch sued a lawyer for unpaid subscription fees; he cross-claimed it was an uncertified lawyer referral service; trial court said no, Court of Appeal reversed. LegalMatch became State Bar-certified in California in Sept 2020 (rules include malpractice insurance, audits, low-income access, registration fees up to $10,000/yr). Model: free to public, lawyers pay subscription. — [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2019/a152442.html), [ABA Litigation News](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/litigation/resources/litigation-news/2020/spring/legalmatch-must-be-regulated-lawyer-referral-service/), [SF Chronicle](https://www.sfchronicle.com/bayarea/article/Court-says-online-legal-referral-firm-violated-14867892.php), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LegalMatch)
- UK SRA: solicitors may accept referrals only if the introducer acquired clients permissibly; fee-sharing/referral fees must be disclosed in writing to the client; referral fees prohibited for personal injury (LASPO); payment varying per lead suggests it is a referral payment not marketing and the solicitor must be satisfied it is reasonable. — [SRA marketing guidance](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/guidance/marketing-public/), [SRA LASPO guidance](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/guidance/prohibition-of-referral-fees-in-laspo-56-60/), [JBL Compliance](https://jblcompliance.com/solicitor-referral-fees-sra-rules/)
- SRA warning notice on marketing flags unsolicited approaches (cold calling) especially in high-volume consumer claims. — [JBL Compliance](https://jblcompliance.com/the-sras-new-warning-notice-on-marketing-what-law-firms-need-to-know/)

### Inferences
- Lesson: transaction-linked fees (per matter, % of fee) and platform "vouching" are the failure mode; flat subscription/advertising is the survivor model. Singapore rule 39(2) is broader on "any consideration", so Singapore may be stricter than UK on lead payments.
- Rating/review features (Avvo ratings) survived; fixed-fee bundles did not.

### Gaps
- Find a Lawyer UK, Australia (state law society referral rules): not researched.
- Avvo's "Rule 7.2" specifics not retrieved; sourced only via state opinions above.
- Current Martindale-Avvo pricing: not retrieved.

## Comparable Singapore verticals (renovation portals)

### Takeaway
Qanvast (acquired by Livspace Dec 2021) monetises through designer subscription packages and credits used to buy leads; Renonation matches homeowners to firms with reviews. Both are lead-gen models free to consumers, which would face compliance limits if copied to lawyers.

### Cited Findings
- Qanvast: free for consumers; earns by charging interior designers a subscription package to advertise; in the current model IDs use purchased credits to buy leads. Present in Singapore, Malaysia, Hong Kong. — search summary of Vulcan Post, Yahoo, others: [Vulcan Post](https://vulcanpost.com/721133/qanvast-match-homeowners-interior-designers-singapore/), [Yahoo](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/home-renovation-woes-begone-qanvast-aims-transparent-hyper-112222085.html)
- Livspace acquired a majority stake in Qanvast, 29 Dec 2021. — [Tradelink Media](https://www.tradelinkmedia.biz/publications/6/news/3438), [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/qanvast/__FEc0GmzmP1V22U43VauFEGyGY7zaShxfH14bW6xmluA)
- Revenue estimates conflict: one aggregator shows SGD 1M-10M (Mar 2023); another states $127.3M for 2026, which is implausible for Qanvast alone and likely mislabeled (possibly group-level). Unreliable. — [Owler](https://www.owler.com/company/qanvast)
- Renonation: leading Singapore renovation/ID directory with ratings and reviews; "thousands" of homeowners paired (self-claim). Monetisation not found. — [renonation.sg](https://www.renonation.sg/)
- Singapore ID economics: many charge ~10% design fee; "free design" packages recover cost via 20-45% markups (blog claims, not rigorous). — [Qanvast article](https://qanvast.com/sg/articles/how-interior-design-firms-make-money-from-your-renovation-1831), [ACE blog](https://www.ace-interiordesign.com/post/how-interior-designers-charge-in-singapore)
- Other Singapore renovation platforms: RenoTender. — [RenoTender](https://www.renotender.com.sg/)

### Inferences
- Subscription plus credit-based lead purchase is the proven renovation model; the lawyer analogue of pay-per-lead is the contested piece under rule 39(2).
