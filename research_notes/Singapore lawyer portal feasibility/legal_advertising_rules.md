## 1. What PCR 2015 Part 5 (publicity/touting) permits and prohibits

### Takeaway
Publicity by lawyers is permitted (including press, TV, internet) but is governed by Part 5 of the Legal Profession (Professional Conduct) Rules 2015 (S 706/2015) — NOT "Part 7" as the brief guessed. Core limits: no touting (r 39/43(4)), no misleading/unverifiable/expectation-inflating content, nothing unbefitting the profession's dignity (r 44), specialisation claims must be justifiable (r 43). Fee-price lists and disparaging other firms' fees are treated as improper.

### Cited Findings
- Publicity on the Internet is subject to Part 5 of the PCR 2015, which governs publicity in or outside Singapore — [Law Society GN 6.1.1 para 30](https://law-society-singapore-prod.s3.ap-southeast-1.amazonaws.com/2020/03/121.-Ethics-and-Information-Technology-GN-6.1.1.pdf)
- A law practice website may be used as an advertising tool or to give generic legal information; giving legal advice online creates attendant obligations; disclaimers advised — [GN 6.1.1 para 31](https://law-society-singapore-prod.s3.ap-southeast-1.amazonaws.com/2020/03/121.-Ethics-and-Information-Technology-GN-6.1.1.pdf)
- Rule 44(1)(a): publicity must not be "likely to diminish public confidence in the legal profession or otherwise bring it into disrepute"; r 44(1)(b): not "unbefitting the dignity of the legal profession" and (as applied by the Ethics Committee) not reasonably regarded as misleading — [Law Society PD 6.2.1](https://law-society-singapore-prod.s3.ap-southeast-1.amazonaws.com/2020/03/66.-Advertisement-and-Media-Publicity-PD-6.2.1.pdf)
- Press/TV advertisements are not touting, as they lack direct in-person solicitation; flyers distributed in public places are different — PD 6.2.1 Part B (same link)
- Ethics Committee view: an ad may be misleading if it (a) has a material misrepresentation (e.g. claiming to be a "leading family law practice" without expertise), (b) omits a material fact, (c) contains unverifiable information (e.g. only a phone number, no firm name), or (d) creates unjustified expectation of results (e.g. promising to recover party-and-party costs) — PD 6.2.1 Part D (same link)
- Unbefitting dignity: suggesting other practices overcharge, or setting out price lists; but generally referring to fixed-fee arrangements is OK; fee disclosure duty under r 17 best discharged personally — PD 6.2.1 Part D
- Filming at a practice: name of the practice should not appear in scenes because that may reasonably be regarded as touting under r 43(4); name in closing credits is OK — PD 6.2.1 Part C
- Complimentary newspaper advertising is permitted if rr 43 and 44 are complied with; description of specialisation must accord with r 43(1)(a) and 43(2) — PD 6.2.1 Part E
- Rule 43(4) [SEARCH-SUMMARY, from a 2001-era Law Gazette commentary, may relate to predecessor rule]: touting is undefined; a regular use of a third party to bring work that fails referral criteria could lead to inference of touting — v1.lawgazette.com.sg (not read)
- Rule numbering from search snippets [SEARCH-SUMMARY, unverified]: r 38 business/trade/calling not derogating from dignity; r 39 touting and referrals; r 40 agreements for referral of conveyancing; r 41 publicity in accordance with rules; r 43 practice info/publicity content; r 44 misleading/inaccurate/unbefitting; r 45 contributions to good causes; r 46 third-party publicity; r 47 free legal advice — sso.agc.gov.sg/SL/LPA1966-S706-2015 (blocked; snippets only)
- Claims reported in snippets [SEARCH-SUMMARY, low confidence, from a law-firm blog ghostlinelegal.com not read]: success rates cannot be advertised; comparisons with other firms' fees/quality not allowed; client references prohibited if confidentiality breached.

### Inferences
- Superlatives/rankings/"best lawyer" claims, testimonials and success-rate claims: I found no primary text. Based on r 44 and PD 6.2.1(D), unverifiable "leading/best" claims are high-risk for the lawyer. A portal's own editorial "top lawyer" rankings would be hard for lawyers to republish and could make them complicit if they approve/participate (r 46 third-party publicity - text not read).
- Where a portal's content is about a lawyer and the lawyer participates, the lawyer is likely responsible for ensuring it complies (PD 6.2.1 Part C wording: every practitioner is responsible for compliance of filming/publicity).

### Gaps
- Verbatim text of rr 38-47 and any amendments (PCR 2015 has been amended since; e.g., 2020s amendments unchecked). Need SSO.
- Whether testimonials, awards/rankings, fee advertising have specific prohibitions; no source read.
- Whether Part 5 was renumbered or changed (e.g., liberalisation of publicity rules) in 2020-2026: unknown.

## 2. Referral fees, pay-per-lead, subscription, pay-per-click

### Takeaway
Paying (or sharing fees with) a referrer for client leads is prohibited: PCR r 39(2) and LPA s 83(2)(d)/(e). The Law Society says participation in an internet referral scheme that requires the law practice to pay a fee or share fees "would be a breach of the LPA". Even free introduction services are improper if they "unfairly attract work". Pure advertising (fixed-fee listing, flat subscription, arguably PPC for publicity) is not expressly addressed; the Law Society itself sells paid "Featured Lawyer" listings.

### Cited Findings
- Rule 39(2) PCR: for third-party referrals, the practitioner must not accept from the referror payment of commission/referral fee/other consideration, nor reward the referror by commission, referral fee or other consideration; must communicate directly with client to obtain/confirm instructions (r 39(2)(g)); must not accept instructions from the third party; must not leave blank Warrants to Act with the third party — [PD 7.4.3](https://law-society-singapore-prod.s3.ap-southeast-1.amazonaws.com/2020/03/35.-Warrant-To-Act-Letter-of-Engagement-and-Referrals-from-Third-Parties-PD-7.4.3.pdf) and [GN 6.1.1 para 34](https://law-society-singapore-prod.s3.ap-southeast-1.amazonaws.com/2020/03/121.-Ethics-and-Information-Technology-GN-6.1.1.pdf)
- LPA s 83(2)(d) and (e) (as cited in GN 6.1.1 para 33): improper conduct/offence for a solicitor to tender, give or consent to retention out of fee of any gratification for having procured employment, or directly/indirectly procure employment through someone to whom remuneration for obtaining it was given/promised — GN 6.1.1 para 33
- "There are prohibitions against a law practice rewarding any person for referring work to them. The participation in any Internet referral schemes which requires the law practice to pay a fee or share fees paid for legal services referred would be a breach of the LPA." — GN 6.1.1 para 35
- "Even if no fees are paid or shared, any participation in an online introduction service or referral service carried out in such a way as to 'unfairly attract work' to the law practice would be improper given... s 83(2)(b) and/or s 83(2)(h) of the LPA." — GN 6.1.1 para 36
- Council ruling: improper for a law practice to demand a referral fee from another law practice for merely referring work ("brokering") — GN 6.1.1 para 37
- Council extends r 40 conventions (written agreement; referror must comply with PCR; no commission either way; immediate termination right; referror's publicity must not suggest service is free, differential pricing, or tying other services; referror must not impair client's free choice) from conveyancing referrals to non-injury/personal-injury motor accident referrals — PD 7.4.3 Part D para 2
- Rule 40 (conveyancing referral agreements) requires written agreement; referror undertakes to comply with the Rules; practice may terminate if breach — [SEARCH-SUMMARY], consistent with PD 7.4.3.
- Law Society's own "Find A Featured Lawyer/Law Firm" listings are paid for by advertising and are non-exhaustive — [Law Society page via search snippet](https://www.lawsociety.org.sg/find-a-featured-lawyer-law-firm/) (page not read) — shows paid, flat-fee placement of lawyers is accepted at least when run by the Society.

### Inferences
- Pay-per-lead / pay-per-enquiry / pay-per-matter or % of fee: very likely a breach by the lawyer (r 39(2), s 83(2)(d)/(e), GN 6.1.1 para 35). Portal should not use these models; success-fee or revenue-share with lawyers is the highest risk.
- Fixed-fee listing or flat subscription not tied to leads/outcomes: not expressly prohibited in anything I read; best characterised as advertising rather than referral reward — but GN para 36 ("unfairly attract work") and r 43(4) touting inference remain risks. Arguably safest model: flat, equal, transparent fee, no per-lead tracking, no exclusivity, ranking not purchasable or clearly labelled as advertising.
- Pay-per-click: no guidance found. A per-click charge is a payment correlated with leads, so risk sits between flat subscription and pay-per-lead. UNCERTAIN; recommend written clarification from the Law Society Ethics Committee/MinLaw.
- Portal must not take instructions, handle Warrants to Act, collect fees or hold client money on behalf of lawyers (PD 7.4.3 para 1; r 39(2)(g)).

### Gaps
- Direct text of r 39(2)(a)-(g), s 83(2)(b),(h). No published ruling specific to pay-per-click or subscription directories found.
- Whether the LPA 2020 Rev Ed renumbered s 83, s 33 etc.

## 3. Law Society/MinLaw guidance on online directories, lead-gen platforms

### Takeaway
No guidance specifically on third-party directories or comparison sites found. The only on-point instrument is GN 6.1.1 Part F ("Online Referral and Introduction Schemes", 1 June 2018), plus PD 7.4.3 and PD 6.2.1.

### Cited Findings
- GN 6.1.1 covers e-mail, practising law on the internet, publicity, and online referral and introduction schemes — [GN 6.1.1](https://law-society-singapore-prod.s3.ap-southeast-1.amazonaws.com/2020/03/121.-Ethics-and-Information-Technology-GN-6.1.1.pdf)
- Law practices must have a physical place of business; "virtual office" entirely online not allowed (GN paras 25-26). A portal cannot be the lawyer's "practice".
- Independent directories exist in Singapore: SgFindLawyer says featured lawyers must hold a current practising certificate verified with the Law Society; SingaporeLegalAdvice.com markets as a portal connecting people to lawyers — [SgFindLawyer](https://sgfindlawyer.com/about/), [SingaporeLegalAdvice](https://singaporelegaladvice.com/) (search snippets only; sites blocked; their business models/fees NOT verified)
- Official directories: Legal Services Regulatory Authority (MinLaw) "Search for a Lawyer or Law Practice" lists practising certificate holders — [search snippet, ask.gov.sg/MinLaw](https://ask.gov.sg/mlaw/questions/cmf0tebqd01gved2h70h0p9gx) (not read)
- MinLaw has a "Relevant Legislation and Communications" page for law practice entities — [MinLaw](https://www.mlaw.gov.sg/law-practice-entities-and-lawyers/resources-for-law-practice-entities/relevant-legislation-and-communications/) (not read)

### Inferences
- Directories that only list verified, publicly available practising-certificate data and neutral practice info are lowest risk; ranking/"best" labels, lead-forwarding with fees, or matching engines are higher risk.

### Gaps
- Any 2019-2026 Law Society circulars on directories, comparison sites, Google Ads, review sites; Law Gazette "Ethics in Practice" columns on directories. Not accessible.
- Any MinLaw/LSRA statements.

## 4. Risk to the portal operator (non-lawyer)

### Takeaway
The PCR bind lawyers, not the portal, but the portal risks (a) aiding lawyers' breaches, (b) LPA s 33 if it gives legal advice or acts in legal matters, (c) general consumer-protection and data-protection law. I found no case against a directory operator.

### Cited Findings
- LPA s 33 (unauthorised person acting as advocate/solicitor or providing legal services); "unauthorised person" defined s 32(2); example: dispatching legal demand letters via a third party breaches s 33 — GN 6.1.1 para 32. Unauthorised-practice overview: [Law Gazette feature](https://lawgazette.com.sg/feature/unauthorised-practice-of-law-what-constitutes-acting-or-practising-as-a-solicitor/) (snippet only; criminal sanctions per snippet).
- LPA s 83(2)(e) targets the solicitor who procures work via someone paid to obtain it — GN para 33; the lawyer is the target, not the introducer.
- CPFTA: misleading/false claims and fake reviews are unfair practices; CCCS enforces; a business undertook to stop fake five-star reviews — [Rajah & Tann via search](https://www.rajahtannasia.com/viewpoints/consumer-protection-in-singapore-five-key-observations-for-businesses-in-a-heightened-enforcement-climate/); [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/report/singapore-unfair-trade-practice-of-fake-reviews/) (snippets). Law Gazette 2004 article on CPFTA and lawyers exists: [link](https://v1.lawgazette.com.sg/2004-5/May04-feature.htm) (not read). Note CPFTA applies to suppliers dealing with consumers; whether a directory's statements fall in is a general-law question.
- PDPA: 11 obligations incl. consent, purpose limitation, notification, data breach notification, accountability, data portability — [Chambers 2026 guide via search](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/comparison/1129/18520/29027-29028-29029-29030-29031) (snippet). Need DPO, consent for consumer enquiries; exceptions for publicly available data not verified.
- Advertising Code/ASAS: searched only indirectly; NOT verified. 

### Inferences
- Operator should avoid: legal advice, document drafting, forwarding "instructions", collecting fees for lawyers, "we will get you the best lawyer" claims, undisclosed paid ranking, fake/solicited-incentivised reviews.
- Contract with lawyers should place PCR compliance on the lawyer, give them approval of their listing content, and disclaim advice.
- Unclear whether a portal could be treated as "touting" itself or abetting (LPA abetment provisions not checked) — flag for legal opinion.

### Gaps
- LPA s 35 (not examined), s 107 (brief mentioned touting; unverified — GN cites s 83(2) not s 107), abetment liability; SAC/ASAS Singapore Code of Advertising Practice applicability; PDPC guidelines on directories/publicly available data; no enforcement vs. portals found.
